An epidemiological model for Zika virus was extracted from the provided scientific paper sections and figure descriptions. The model is a vector-host compartmental model, using an SEIR (Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Recovered) structure for the human population and an SEI (Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious) structure for the adult female mosquito (vector) population. It also incorporates a dynamic vector population by modeling the key life-cycle stages of the mosquito: Egg, Larva, and Pupa.

The model captures the following key dynamics:
1.  **Human Population:** Susceptible individuals are exposed to the virus through bites from infectious mosquitoes. After an incubation period, they become infectious and can later recover, gaining immunity. The model includes natural birth (recruitment) and death rates for the human population.
2.  **Vector Population:** The model includes a full life-cycle for mosquitoes, from eggs to larvae, to pupae, and finally to susceptible adults. Susceptible adult female mosquitoes become exposed after biting an infectious human. Following an extrinsic incubation period, they become infectious and remain so for life. Each vector stage has its own mortality rate.
3.  **Transmission:** The model uses contact-based flows to represent the two transmission pathways: from infectious vectors to susceptible humans (`I_v -> S_h`) and from infectious humans to susceptible vectors (`I_h -> S_v`).

The resulting model is represented in a parametric format, defining all state variables, transitions, and symbolic parameters (e.g., biting rate `b`, transmission probabilities `βₕ`, `βᵥ`, recovery rate `γₕ`) as specified by the metamodel.
