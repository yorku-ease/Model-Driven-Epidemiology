An epidemiological model for Zika virus transmission between humans and mosquitoes was extracted, prioritizing the provided LaTeX equations. The model uses an SEIR structure for the human population (Susceptible, Exposed, Infectious, Recovered) and an SEI structure for adult female mosquitoes, complemented by three preceding life stages: eggs, larvae, and pupae.

The human population follows logistic growth dynamics, characterized by a birth rate into the susceptible compartment and a density-dependent death rate affecting all human compartments. The vector population dynamics are more detailed, modeling the entire life cycle from egg to adult. Egg-laying is proportional to the adult female mosquito population. The larval stage includes both natural and density-dependent mortality, reflecting competition for resources. The pupal stage maturation into adult mosquitoes is split, with only a fraction (`q`) becoming female mosquitoes tracked by the model; the male portion is removed from the system.

Transmission is bidirectional: infectious mosquitoes infect susceptible humans, and infectious humans infect susceptible mosquitoes. The transmission rate is frequency-dependent, influenced by the total human population and the number of alternative hosts for mosquito bites.

All parameters, including base rates and complex rate expressions, were identified from the equations and populated with values and descriptions from the provided tables and text, using the "Rio de Janeiro State" scenario as the reference.
