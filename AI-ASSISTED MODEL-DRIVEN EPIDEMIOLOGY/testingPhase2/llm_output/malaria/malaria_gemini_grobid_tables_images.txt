An extended SEIR-type model for malaria transmission incorporating both vector-borne and non-vector-borne pathways. The model includes two interacting populations: humans and mosquitoes.

The human population is divided into seven compartments: Susceptible (Sₕ), Vaccinated (Vₕ), Exposed via vector transmission (Eₕ₁), Exposed via non-vector transmission (Eₕ₂), Infectious (Iₕ), Treated (Tₕ), and Recovered (Rₕ). New individuals enter the population at a rate π, with a fraction 'k' being vaccinated at birth and entering Vₕ, while the rest (1-k) enter Sₕ. Susceptible and vaccinated individuals can become infected through two routes: contact with infectious mosquitoes (Iₘ), leading to the Eₕ₁ compartment, or through non-vector routes via contact with infectious humans (Iₕ), leading to the Eₕ₂ compartment. The model accounts for reduced susceptibility in vaccinated individuals through a vaccine efficacy parameter (ε). Individuals in both exposed classes progress to the infectious state (Iₕ). Infectious individuals can then move to the treated compartment (Tₕ), and subsequently to the recovered class (Rₕ). The model includes waning immunity from both vaccination (Vₕ to Sₕ) and natural infection (Rₕ to Sₕ).

The mosquito (vector) population is modeled with three compartments: Susceptible (Sₘ), Exposed (Eₘ), and Infectious (Iₘ). Susceptible mosquitoes become exposed by feeding on infectious humans (Iₕ), and after a latent period, they become infectious (Iₘ), capable of transmitting the parasite back to humans.

The model incorporates natural mortality rates for both human (μ₁) and mosquito (μ₂) populations, as well as a disease-induced death rate (δ₁) for infectious and treated humans.
